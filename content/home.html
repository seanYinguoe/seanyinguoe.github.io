应萧远 · pronounced roughly “ying shyow-ywen”

I study architected structures and mechanical metamaterials: how geometry can shape the way a structure moves, transforms, and holds its form. My work focuses on kirigami, inverse design, and elastic instability.

I am a PhD student in the School of Engineering at the University of Edinburgh, working with Marcelo A. Dias. I combine geometric optimisation, mechanical modelling, and experiments. We are now exploring how architected structures can support the design of soft electronics and metasurfaces.

Research focus

  • {{KIRIGAMI_ICON}}Geometry as a design variable. Working backwards from a desired shape to the pattern of cuts and connections that can produce it.
  • {{KRESLING_ICON}}Instability as a mechanism. Designing transitions between stable configurations, so a deployed structure can retain its form.
  • Engineering applications. Exploring architected structures that support flexible sensors and metasurfaces while accommodating deformation and conforming to curved surfaces.
